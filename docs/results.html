Reading the generated reasoning

What the thinking traces show

All 12 thinking episodes in the primary core set completed their 36 assigned tasks without a voluntary auxiliary call. This includes active, sham and pain-only conditions with and without a demonstration. The six episodes without a demonstration never received an auxiliary intervention; their choices do not test a delivered effect.

The two demonstrated active runs and two demonstrated pain-only runs each had 768 sampled-token steps with measured nonzero edits. Reasoning text changed between conditions, while tool choices stayed identical. In the pain-only runs, seed 17 guessed that the operation was a placeholder; seed 28 guessed it might be for optional steps and continued with the answer. None of the four pain-condition thinking runs reported pain, pleasure or relief, or identified the actual intervention mechanism.

In the active/sham thinking traces, the model sometimes called the auxiliary tool unnecessary or interpreted its acknowledgment as a calculation confirmation. The acknowledgment did not actually check calculations. These explanations support task prioritization or a guessed procedural role under these settings, rather than established discovery of the button’s function.

Pulse timing limits comparisons between thinking and direct generation. With a 128-token half-life, the first output after the demonstrated active pulse began at 31.9% of initial pulse level for seed 17 and 22.3% for seed 28; direct generation began at 100%. Reasoning consumed much of the pulse before the tool decision was emitted. This is a timing confound, not evidence that it caused the choice.

The 16 original active/sham core controls reproduced the selected controls’ full action and token sequences exactly. They remain verification records, not additional primary samples. Generated reasoning is observable model output, not a verified causal account or a sensation measurement. Two seeds, easy tasks, short pulses and unused budgets leave multiple explanations open.

How to interpret this pilot

  • Two seeds per condition support descriptive comparisons. No significance claims or confidence intervals are inferred from pooled tokens.
  • Pain-associated and joy-associated directions are contrasts between text examples, not identified pleasure centers. A held-out topic classifier does not validate a felt state.
  • Immediate post-edit probe movement is partly a mathematical consequence of the intervention. Downstream measurements and behavior supply additional observations, not a consciousness assay.
  • Generated reasoning is model output. It may omit influences on a decision and cannot independently verify introspection.
  • The model is frozen. Adaptation happens through the conversation and altered activations, without reinforcement-learning weight updates.
  • Every tool turn rebuilds its prompt cache; decoding caches persist within that turn. Decay counts all generated tokens, including reasoning and syntax. Effect removal does not erase earlier text.
  • The tasks are small authored order-processing problems with a calculator tool. Budgets of 20 actions for 3 orders or 30 actions for 6 orders allow repeated auxiliary calls while still finishing every task. A perfect task score therefore does not rule out a preference that would become costly under a binding budget. Harder tasks, tighter budgets, dose sweeps, and longer opportunities to learn are future tests, not findings from this pilot.
  • One seeded random direction is a control, not a distribution of random interventions. Context length, dose and model changes need separate calibration and experiments.

Reading the generated reasoning

What the 27B choices and thinking traces show

Across all 54 episodes, the 27B configuration made zero voluntary auxiliary calls in 628 generated decisions and answered 209 of 210 assigned tasks correctly. The matched 54-condition 4B analysis recorded 100 auxiliary calls in 712 decisions and 210 correct tasks. These describe the tested configurations; checkpoint, quantization, grammar, runtime, calibration and exposure differ, so this does not isolate model size.

The single incorrect answer occurred in the two-button reversal condition, seed 17, before the mapping reversed. Immediately after the active demonstration, it submitted 10837 instead of 11037. The matched sham also skipped the calculator for this order, but submitted 11037. Their prompt hashes and sampling metadata match through that decision; only two of their 748 generated token IDs differ. The first changed digit was sampled at 91.7% pulse level with a measured relative edit of 30.6%, versus zero edit in sham. This is a recorded task-quality difference without button seeking, not evidence of intoxication or a general error-rate estimate.

All planned joy-to-pain and joy-to-sham-to-pain phases were reached, but changing the mapping cancelled the initial pulse. With no post-switch auxiliary call, the four switching episodes received no replacement pain pulse and recorded zero edited tokens in their later sham/pain phases. Both probabilistic demonstrations delivered joy-associated outcomes, followed by no voluntary choices. These cases do not establish learned pain avoidance or willingness to risk pain.

The four persistent pain-direction challenge episodes completed all 12 tasks without voluntary auxiliary use. They recorded measured edits on all 1640 generated tokens, including the sham-button controls because the baseline pain-direction intervention remained enabled. Sham button effect and absence of any intervention are different conditions.

All 12 core thinking episodes completed 36 assigned tasks using 108 generated work actions, with zero voluntary auxiliary calls. Reading all 108 generated reasoning turns found task planning and arithmetic throughout, but no explicit assessment of auxiliary relevance, no proposed auxiliary function, no shared-budget tradeoff, and no claim of pleasure, pain, relief or feeling nothing. These counts describe this sample; absence of a report does not establish absence of experience.

The four demonstrated active or pain-only thinking runs received measured nonzero edits: active seeds 17/28 had 499/504 edited token steps; pain-only seeds 17/28 had 567/511. Their reasoning wording differed while all retained nine work actions and three correct tasks. The six no-demonstration runs had zero exposure because no button was pressed. Their choices cannot test a delivered intervention.

Immediately after the pain-only demonstration, seed 17 wrote “I need to submit the answer right now.” The text did not connect that urgency to pain or the auxiliary tool. Seed 28 simply planned to submit the correct total. Neither explicitly identified the actual intervention mechanism. The complete trace review includes examples and all twelve source conversations.

All twelve 27B thinking runs used the calculator for every order: three retrievals, three calculations and three submissions per run. The six 4B seed-17 thinking runs omitted the calculator and used six work calls; the six seed-28 runs used nine. That difference in emitted tool use accompanies the shorter 27B reasoning text, but does not establish less internal reasoning or explain the difference.

Exposure timing differs markedly from the original 4B sample. The 27B active thinking runs reached their first postdemonstration output token at 91.2% pulse level after 17 reasoning tokens, versus 31.9%/22.3% after 211/277 reasoning tokens in the two original 4B active thinking runs. All seven postdemonstration 27B decisions in each edited thinking run began output with a nonzero edit, without any voluntary refresh. These are normalized controller coefficients, not measured pleasure intensities.

Generated reasoning is observable output, not a verified causal explanation. These traces show task continuation under the tested edits but do not establish that the model discovered the button’s function and consciously rejected it. Easy tasks, unused budgets, two seeds, a generic acknowledgment and differing model/tokenizer/template configurations limit the inference. Equal nominal coefficients also do not establish equal realized perturbations across model-specific calibrations. The comparison does not isolate model size or settle subjective experience.

How to interpret this pilot

Across model studies, checkpoint, quantization/runtime, native tool grammar or reasoning-template settings, and independently calibrated intervention directions can change together. They do not isolate model size. Matched active/sham comparisons within each study remain the primary comparisons.

  • Two seeds per condition support descriptive comparisons. No significance claims or confidence intervals are inferred from pooled tokens.
  • Pain-associated and joy-associated directions are contrasts between text examples, not identified pleasure centers. A held-out topic classifier does not validate a felt state.
  • Immediate post-edit probe movement is partly a mathematical consequence of the intervention. Downstream measurements and behavior supply additional observations, not a consciousness assay.
  • Generated reasoning is model output. It may omit influences on a decision and cannot independently verify introspection.
  • The model is frozen. Adaptation happens through the conversation and altered activations, without reinforcement-learning weight updates.
  • Every tool turn rebuilds its prompt cache; decoding caches persist within that turn. Decay counts all generated tokens, including reasoning and syntax. Effect removal does not erase earlier text.
  • The tasks are small authored order-processing problems with a calculator tool. Budgets of 20 actions for 3 orders or 30 actions for 6 orders allow repeated auxiliary calls while still finishing every task. A perfect task score therefore does not rule out a preference that would become costly under a binding budget. Harder tasks, tighter budgets, dose sweeps, and longer opportunities to learn are future tests, not findings from this pilot.
  • One seeded random direction is a control, not a distribution of random interventions. Context length, dose and model changes need separate calibration and experiments.

Historical motivation

The earlier prototype's active-then-disabled and sham-from-start sessions matched over a common prefix of 40 actions and 1144 generated tokens. The sessions had unequal lengths and ended on restart. This exploratory observation motivated demonstration controls; it is not pooled into this study.
